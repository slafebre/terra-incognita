# Developing Terra Incognita

Notes for working on the generator: debugging parameters, reproducing a view, and the command-line dev tools. For how the generator works, see [SPEC.md](../SPEC.md).

## Debugging parameters

`?debug` shows chunk stages and timings, `?at=x,y` sets the start position (world metres), `?z=0.5` sets the start zoom, `?R=0.9` forces the region everywhere (0 = lowland … 1 = alpine), `?seed=7` makes the world reproducible (seeds hashed from position instead of fresh), for comparing changes on the same land, and `?workers=n` sets the worker pool size.

## Reproducing a view

Press `i` (or add `?info`) for the reproduction panel: everything needed to rebuild the land on screen in the browser and in the dev tools. With `?seed=n` that is a link and a `SEED=n` command line. Emergent land needs its seeds: **Download snapshot** saves every seed held (about 0.6 MB) with the view, the forced region and the commit. Put the file in `dev/snaps/`, then `?snap=/dev/snaps/<file>` replays it in the browser (other parameters still win) and `SNAP=dev/snaps/<file>` replays it in `dev/spot.ts` (position defaults to the snapshot's) and `dev/towns.ts` (plans the settlement nearest the view). The same seeds only give the same land on the same code, so a snapshot made at another commit warns.

## Tests

```
npm test
```

Tests make their land from world seed 7 and print the seed they used; `SEED=n npm test` uses other land and `SEED=random npm test` new land every run, so a failure on random land can be run again with its seed. `test/golden.test.ts` checks a digest of every task's output round the first golden place against `test/golden/seed7-place0.json`; after a change meant to alter the land, `npm run golden` rewrites that file (say in the commit what changed), and `dev/golden.ts` (below) shows by how much.

## Dev tools

- `/dev/overview.html?size=24000` renders a 24 km overview of the un-eroded terrain, tinted by region type.
- `npx vite-node dev/probe.ts` drives the whole pipeline in-process (no workers) and prints per-task timings.
- `npx vite-node dev/coverstats.ts [areas] [R,R,…]` reports land cover percentages per region type (for calibration).
- `npx vite-node dev/netstats.ts [cells] [R,R,…]` reports way density per class (N, D, C, track, footpath) and site density from net cells alone (fast, no fine terrain). `SEED=n` picks the cells and seeds the world reproducibly, to compare changes.
- `SEED=n npx vite-node dev/spot.ts x y [R or -] [radius]` builds the pipeline around one place, seeded as the app's `?seed=n`, and lists the ways drawn near it. `SNAP=<file>` replays a snapshot instead (x y optional). `SVG=<file>` draws the land within the radius (5 m contours, streams, lakes, every way; routed tracks and footpaths in red); `GRADES=1` reports each class's mean grade and share over its limit, measured on the DEM.
- `OUT=<file> npx vite-node dev/golden.ts` runs the pipeline in-process around five seeded places (the region field, and lowland to alpine forced) and saves every task's output; `GOLD=<file> npx vite-node dev/golden.ts` compares with such a file, field by field (identical, or how many elements differ and by how much), and with its per-stage timings. For changes that should leave the land as it is (optimisations); `PLACES=n` uses the first n places, `SEED=n` another world seed.
- `npx vite-node dev/netbench.ts [block] [R]` times a block of neighbouring net cells built in turn, as a worker does (the first cold, then warm).
- `npx vite-node dev/seams.ts [firstSeed] [runs] [R]` runs the pipeline test's seam check with seeded `Math.random` (optionally with the region forced to R), so a bad run can be reproduced; prints the worst chunk border lines of runs above 1.3×.
- `npx vite-node dev/roadstats.ts [areas] [R,R,…]` runs the whole pipeline on seeded, reproducible areas (`SEED=n`) and reports per region type: small closed loops, crossings away from junctions, length running alongside another road, dead ends, unreached settlements, and the share of each class steeper than its limit on the fine terrain. Options: `MEASURE=<file>` (write the per-region rates for `dev/check.ts` to judge), `SHEET=<prefix>` (SVG contact sheet of the loops), `DUMP=<prefix>` (SVG of each area), `DEAD=<prefix>` (sheet of dead ends), `DETOURS=1` (main-road detours with coarse vs fine gradient), `GRADECMP=1` (coarse vs fine heights and gradients), `HAIRPINS=<prefix>` (sheet of hairpins over 5 m contours; their apex radius, leg spacing and length are always reported), `PINLOG=1` (hairpin positions), `LOOPZOOM=<prefix>` (close-ups of small loops with every vertex), `LOOPLOG=1` (each small loop's position and the ways around it), `LOOP_HA` (loop size threshold, ha). It also reports road in lakes per class (crossings vs shore clips), way vertices under water, ways ending in or within 24 m of a lake, and lattice edges no chunk draws (paths are sampled every 8 m); `WET=<prefix>` (sheet of the crossings, lakes shaded), `WETLOG=1` (their positions as `?at=`), `LAKECMP=1` (lakes roads see vs lakes drawn), `HASHSEED=n` (seeds hashed from position, as the app's `?seed=n`, to look at a place in the browser), `COARSELOG=1` (positions of coarse-basin lakes); the `COARSE LAKES` line says how much of the lake area, wet road and wet vertices lie in the lakes of closed coarse basins.
  - This tool measures; `dev/check.ts` judges (`MEASURE=<file>` here writes the rates, `ROADS=<file>` there holds them against the budgets in `dev/judge.ts`). A run on its own prints its rates and exits 0, so there is one place that says yes or no over the network's defects and the seams together. Eight counts carry a budget: dead ends, small closed loops, ways standing in a lake over 30 m (`wet-run`), ways ending in one (`way-in-lake`), lattice edges at water no chunk draws, settlements no road reaches, micro-piece paths over 1.5× their chord, and road over 1.5× its class limit.
  - Budgets are **per region type** and on the **rate**, not the count. Per region because the rates move with the land by more than a regression would — micro detours ~10 per 100 km in the lowlands against ~46 in the alpine, steep road 2.5 % against 13 % — so one number for all four could only be the alpine's and would pass a fourfold lowland regression. On the rate so a longer run measures the same map more closely instead of failing for being longer. Two denominators, named in each row: counts the tool clips to the area go against the road inside it, counts taken over every drawn piece against all the road drawn.
  - A region with under `JUDGE_KM` (80) km of road in its areas is reported and **not** judged (by `check.ts`, which holds that gate), so a quick look can't fail for being quick: one 3 km area holds ~47 km and its loop rate lands within 3 % of the budget by luck alone. Three areas give a region 100–150 km. The `LOOKED AT` line says how many regions had road enough.
  - `way-in-lake` and `unreached-site` are 0 — the spec gives both outright, and 36 areas over three seeds hold them. `way-in-lake` is a guard whose detector is demonstrably alive: `shoreEnds` runs the same code by the same test but for `nearWater` instead of water underfoot, and finds 28 way-ends near water over the same runs. `unreached-site` doubles a Vitest invariant (§7, every settlement connected) on real land across regions, so it guards rather than discovers.
  - The rest are what the generator does today: the worst of three seeds plus a quarter, on three areas per region each. Three samples a region is thin, so a near miss is worth rerunning on another seed before believing it. `wet-run` and `undrawn-at-water` are loose ceilings and nothing more — counts of 0–8 and 0–2 a region that moved severalfold between seeds (one seed's land is simply wetter), and a single undrawn edge in a 100 km region is already ~1 per 100 km, so the ceiling has to tolerate four.
  - Baseline (3 areas × 4 regions, ~515 km of road in 108 km², ~285 settlements; seeds 2, 3 and 4 all pass): dead ends 8.9–23.5 per 100 km in the area, small loops 21.6–45.9, micro detours 9.2–54.0 per 100 km drawn, steep road 2.5–12.7 % (3.4–16.4 % before roads saw the slope between an edge's own vertices and steep pieces were judged edge by edge; its budgets were tightened to match), wet runs 0–6.1, undrawn edges at water 0–1.9, ways ending in a lake and unreached settlements 0 throughout. Loosening the loop threshold (`LOOP_HA=20`) puts `small-loop` over budget in all four regions (92.7 / 81.1 / 74.3 / 62.7 against 37 / 54 / 51 / 42) and exits 1, which is how the budgets were checked to be looking at all.
- `npx vite-node dev/detours.ts [R] [cells]` finds main-road detours in net cells alone and compares the direct lattice edge's cost with the detour's.
- `npx vite-node dev/terraincmp.ts [areas] [R,R,…]` runs the pipeline on seeded areas and compares the coarse surface (what roads and settlements plan on) with the map's: heights and slopes over 256/512 m (with the best a 256 m sampling could do), height differences by drainage area, and the share of filled flats.
- `npx vite-node dev/towns.ts [per region] [R,R,…]` runs the pipeline around villages (`TOWNS=1`: towns, `HAMLETS=1`: hamlets, `FARMS=1`: farmsteads, framed 260 m round each, with counts of the forms chosen and why buildings were refused) found with a world seed (`SEED=n`, same as `?seed=n`) and writes each one's street plan as SVG over the fine terrain, plus a sheet of all (`OUT=<prefix>`). `INPUTS=<file>` caches the plans' inputs, so the plans can be tuned without rerunning the pipeline.
- `npx vite-node dev/fields.ts [km] [R,R,…]` runs the pipeline over a km × km area per region (`SEED=n`), lays out each chunk's field parcels as its render would and writes `<OUT>-R<r>.svg` (`OUT=fields`): woods, water, ways, buildings, field edges and hedges (`LATTICE=1` adds the lattice edges carrying ways, as the blocks see them). It reports field-edge and hedge length per km², blocks cut, blocks too large and furlong pieces, and the layout time per chunk.
- `npx vite-node dev/pois.ts [km] [R,R,…]` runs the pipeline over a km × km area per region (`SEED=n`) and counts the points of interest each chunk draws (hilltop chapels and crosses, triangulation points, wayside crosses, springs, water towers, reservoirs) per km²; `LOG=1` lists them as `?at=` positions.
- `npx vite-node dev/tilespread.ts [R,R,…]` measures how much overlapping coarse tiles disagree, by drainage area.
- `/dev/motorways.html?size=128000&x=..&y=..&seed=n&R=..&raw=1` draws the motorways (experimental, SPEC §3.5.1) over the coarse surface of a large area, with their noise line dashed, ends, towns and villages; `raw=1` also draws the dropped line, coloured by why (gate, alpine, sharp, loop, saddle, stub), and curves tighter than the minimum are ringed (listed in the console).
- `npx vite-node dev/check.ts [areas] [R,R,…]` checks the spec's invariants against the map as it is drawn — what a chunk's render is given (`ChunkManager.drawnInput`), built up as the render builds it (the network's segments the plans did not redraw, plus the plans' own pieces), not a second reckoning of it. On seeded areas (`SEED=n`) stood on a motorway, since a 3 km area picked at random holds none. It reports each check's count with a rate, per 10 km of motorway or per 100 km² of land, and the run ends by saying what it looked at (symbols, plans, buildings, street and ramp length), so a count of 0 can be told from nothing to count.
  - *Motorways* (§3.5.1, §3.8): one ending inside a tunnel; a junction whose lattice vertex is within MW_VERTEX, and one drawn within the snap's own clearance (the two are kept in different places, so they are counted apart); a way within MW_EDGE without crossing, and a crossing shallower than 35° (both measured on the drawn curve, which §256 says crosses more shallowly than its edge); a ramp merging on a viaduct or in a tunnel; a way crossing a ramp without a junction; a symbol on a motorway or a ramp.
  - *The seams between the tiers*: a wayside cross set beyond its junction from the ways' directions there, but inside a drawn curve that bends back over it (`poi-on-way`); a symbol in a building or on a street (`poi-on-building` — `placePois` measures this against the same plans by the same metric, so it is a guard, not a detector); a plan's own symbol against the plans next to it, the motorways and the ramps, none of which `planTown` ever saw (`plan-poi-clear`); and a ramp over a building or a street, which the plan was laid out before (`ramp-building`, `ramp-street`).
  - Counts go against the budgets in `dev/judge.ts` — zero where the invariant is meant to hold outright, open where the spec already records the defect — and it exits non-zero over budget. It is the only run that does: `ROADS=<file>` judges a `roadstats` run's rates in the same pass, and without one it says in as many words that it judged the seams alone, so half a verdict cannot read as a pass. `LOG=1` lists every violation as `?at=`, `SNAP=<file>` checks one snapshot's own land instead of seeded areas (what you saw on the map, put to the same rules), `HASHSEED=n` seeds the world from position so a violation opens in the browser, `FIND=n` how far to look for a motorway to stand an area on (24 km).
  - Baseline, three seeds (`SEED=2,3,4`, 6 areas × 4 regions each: 72 areas, 622 km² of land, 222 km of motorway, 933 symbols and 27 a plan's, 2,199 plans with 29,883 buildings, 81 km of street, 14.7 km of ramp; ~10 min a seed). Every zero-budget check holds on all three, and so do `ramp-building`, `ramp-street` and `plan-poi-clear` over 14.7 km of ramp and 27 plan symbols — better evidence than the first run's 2 km and 6, still not much. Open rates over all three: ways alongside 2.47 per 10 km, ramps crossed without a junction 0.58, ramp merges on a structure 0.31, shallow crossings 0.36. **The alongside figure here is in the old unit** — 8 m cells, and measured before the check's four fixes below; on the `SEED=1,2,3` sample the same land now reads 0.73 per 10 km in incidents. This sample has not been re-measured since.
  - **A count here is comparable only with `HASHSEED` set.** `SEED=n` seeds `Math.random`, which picks where the areas stand; the world itself comes from `freshSeed()` off that same `Math.random`, so a run is reproducible but only against code that asks for its seeds in the same order. The first run recorded here (`SEED=2`, 3 areas × 4 regions, 32.6 km) cannot be reproduced: the same command gives that run's land with different detail on it (2.0 km of ramp against 3.6, 3,800 buildings against 3,712 over the same 329 plans), and nothing committed since can do that, so it was an uncommitted working state. Its 0.31 alongside and 0.92 merges were written up as a sample too short, corrected eightfold by a wider one. That was wrong: the numbers are not comparable, and seed to seed the alongside rate runs 3.99 to 12.59 per 10 km on identical parameters. And it had every check at zero while a real fault sat in the symbols: widening the sample found a wayside cross inside a drawn way on two seeds (§3.8, fixed in `src/gen/poi.ts`, with the case now in `test/poi.test.ts`).
  - `mw-vertex` was wrong twice before it was right, which is the part worth remembering. It first took the lattice vertex nearest each *drawn* junction — unsound, since vertices sit as little as 30 m apart while the snap moves a junction up to VSNAP fine nodes — and reported 41–42 m hits that were not the vertex the ways met at. Reading the lattice edges `drawnInput` now passes (endpoints are the unsnapped vertices) it then reported 12–34 m hits, all 21–25 m from a terminus end: the 75 m of ring and lanes the network deliberately does not see, so that roads may reach the ring (SPEC §3.5.1 Ends, and §Next names the gap). With that stretch left out it holds on all three seeds, and it now tests the rule the network actually enforces — an edge is refused where *either* endpoint is within MW_VERTEX, so a vertex of degree 1 counts, not just a junction.
  - The checks were separately shown to be looking at all by loosening each threshold ~20×, which makes all five seam checks fire on one snapshot (16, 5, 1, 156 and 83 hits).
  - It no longer holds copies of the generator's distances: MW_VERTEX, MW_EDGE, MW_ANGLE and the fit's clearance are imported from `motorway.ts`, which owns every tier's (§3.5.1 A barrier). Both of this tool's own mistakes were copies.
  - `mw-alongside` counted the check's own mistakes more than the map's, and four fixes took it from 104 hits to 8 incidents over `SEED=1,2,3` (36 areas, 303 km², 110 km of motorway): 50/13/41 per seed before, 5/0/3 after, with every other count on all three seeds unchanged. None of them touched the land. They were: let a crossing off by MW_BRIDGE_FREE of where the lattice *chord* crosses, which is the geometry SPEC §3.5.1 grants it, rather than by a radius of 60 m round where the drawn curve crosses; measure against the chains with the terminus approach cut off, as the vertex rule already does, since the network stops seeing a motorway MW_END_CLEAR short of its ring; skip the stretches that are in a tunnel or on a viaduct, where a way passes under or over rather than beside; and report one hit per run alongside instead of one per 8 m cell, with the run's length, so the rate is the same quantity as the per-crossing rates beside it. Each hit also carries the distance to the nearest bridge, which is what makes the remainder readable: all 8 stand 80–144 m out from one, six of them within a metre of MW_BRIDGE_FREE. SPEC §3.5.1 Open has what that leaves. It is not what I said it was: bounding `edgeCostRaw`'s crossing exemption to MW_BRIDGE_FREE changed nothing at all — not one edge over the 110 km, nor at the five golden places — because MW_ANGLE and MW_BRIDGE_FREE already imply MW_EDGE along a straight crossing (30 / sin 35° = 52 m, inside the 80 m bridge). All 8 are the drawing coming closer than the chord it was fitted to, which is the fit's soft 28 m and the fine route's 24 (less near a piece's ends, nothing at a junction).
  - `mw-site` (a hamlet or a farm drawn on a site the network leaves out, MW_SITE) reads 0 over 1,035 hamlet and farm sites on `SEED=1,2,3`, and it is checked by **site id**, not by distance to the plan's centre: a plan's centre is its crossroads and a farmstead's is its vertex, so a distance measured there judges the wrong point. The sites and the motorways are rebuilt from the same seeds (both pure functions of them) and the lookup is given the terminus run-ons `latticeCell` gives it. Shown to be looking: with the threshold ×4 in a scratch copy it fires on farms at 138–249 m, and the nearest farm site to a motorway anywhere in the sample is 138 m — outside the 120 m the rule asks for, which is why the real count is 0 rather than vacuous. The `looked at` line counts the sites it held, and separately any plan whose own site the margin missed, so a 0 cannot come from a check that passed over everything.
  - The level in an `mw-vertex` note was read at the wrong index on a chain whose terminus approach had been cut off, so it could name the wrong tunnel or viaduct. The hit itself was never gated on it, and the count is unaffected.

- `SEED=n npx vite-node dev/mwstats.ts [areas] [km] [R]` measures motorways on seeded areas: length per 100 km², ends per 100 km, tight curves, the share of their profile that is too steep and of their length where the ground lies far off it, and time.
